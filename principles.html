How X-rays are Produced

Electrons are emitted from the cathode and accelerated toward the anode by high voltage. When they strike the tungsten target, X-rays are produced through bremsstrahlung (braking radiation) and characteristic radiation.

The Physics: The maximum X-ray energy equals the electron kinetic energy (E = eV, where e is electron charge, V is voltage). Higher kVp produces more penetrating X-rays with shorter wavelength.

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance

Hydrogen protons (with spin ½) align with the external magnetic field. Radiofrequency pulses at the Larmor frequency flip their spin, and as they relax back, they emit signals used to create detailed images.

The Physics: Larmor equation: ω = γB₀, where γ is gyromagnetic ratio (42.58 MHz/T for hydrogen). Higher field = higher frequency = better signal-to-noise ratio.

Linear Accelerator (LINAC)

High-energy X-rays (photons) or electrons are directed at tumors with millimeter precision. The dose distribution follows a characteristic curve with build-up region, then exponential attenuation.

The Physics: Photon interactions: Photoelectric effect (low energy), Compton scattering (diagnostic range), Pair production (high energy >1.022 MeV). Percentage Depth Dose (PDD) describes energy deposition with depth.
